**A voice for the voiceless.** Mind Interface Company (MIC) builds non-invasive, EEG-based technology that lets people turn their thoughts into text — no surgery required. I'm the founder and CEO.

Site: [mindinterface.co](https://www.mindinterface.co/)

## the problem

People with locked-in syndrome, ALS, and severe stroke can be fully aware and still lose the ability to speak or move. Communication shrinks to whatever residual movement is left — an eye blink, a twitch — and when that goes, so does the channel.

Implanted brain-computer interfaces have shown this can be solved, but they need brain surgery. Most people who need a voice back will never get one that way.

## what we're building

A non-invasive EEG headset paired with a decoding algorithm that reads intended speech and movement. The first goal is restoring communication for locked-in, ALS, and stroke patients, working with Singapore hospitals and research institutions. MIC is in early access in Singapore.

## out in the world

The same BCI work has been showing up outside the lab:

- **[SuperAI 2026](/media/superai-2026-brain-controlled-art)** — a live demo with Tesseract.art where visitors created paintings using their brain activity, driving a robotic painter through attention and engagement signals.
- **[NeuroMech](/apps/neuromech)** — at the Singapore Defense Tech Hackathon 2026, our team put MIC's BCI on a real robot: an operator drove a ground vehicle by gaze alone, decoded from EEG. First of 39 teams.
- **[FUTUREMODE 2026](/media/futuremode-consumer-robotics-era)** — on the panel "The Consumer Robotics Era" in Taipei, on what it takes for robots to reach homes, healthcare, and everyday life.

## why this

My PhD was in biological sciences, and most of what I've built since has been software — fintech, edtech, AI. MIC is where the two meet: the signal is biology, the interface is software, and the person on the other end gets their voice back.
