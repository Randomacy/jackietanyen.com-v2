Written after my first time exhibiting at a conference: a booth in the Accelerator Zone at Seamless Asia in Singapore, where e27 gave us the chance to showcase [fundMyLife](/apps/fundmylife-startup). It's a list of five things the team and I learned, for other first-timers heading into conference season.

1. **Smile and pitch.** Expect people to be standoffish at first. Semi-interested visitors stand a distance away and try to work out what your booth is about, and a big smile and a greeting is often all it takes to bring them in. Every encounter is a mini pitching session, so get the one-liner down pat and watch for the glazed look. Energy is infectious. One visitor looked sceptical the whole first day, then came back on the second with a friend who might be a promising lead.
2. **Pace yourself.** A full day of standing and talking is surprisingly exhausting, especially as an introvert, and the urge to retreat behind the booth is real. If you come as a team, rotate booth duty. That also gives everyone practice pitching.
3. **Decorate your booth and give out free swag.** We started with a plain pod and brought an LED monitor for slideshows, and people stopped to stare at the screen, which is the moment to pitch them. Being scrappy, we stuck our logo on Daiso mints. The whole bowl was gone by lunch, and it filled up with business cards instead. The booths I liked most were Boxgreen, with cute pastel illustrations and free samples, and Cafebond, with tubes of coffee beans and live plants.
4. **Friendship is magic.** The other exhibitors are on similar journeys, and hearing why they started up was inspiring. Our team worked remotely, so the conference was also bonding time for the three of us.
5. **Follow up.** It sounds obvious, but it's surprisingly hard. Companies take seven days on average to follow up on leads from a conference, and by then people have met so many others that your impression has faded. Scribble a note on each name card after you talk to someone, so you can write a personal email afterwards. The pile of cards looks daunting, but recalling each conversation while writing turned out to be a delight.

It ends with thanks to my co-founders Matt and Wesley, and to e27 for the spot in the Accelerator Zone.
